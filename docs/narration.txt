Every month, a small business has to close its books, and the hardest line is payroll. The bank statement shows what left the account — but that single number hides the truth. The real cost of employing your team is far higher than the transfer suggests, and Archon finds that hidden cost automatically, from the raw documents themselves.

A single payroll event leaves three documents, and each tells only part of the story. The bank confirmation shows the net amount that was transferred. The payroll register holds the full employer cost. And the payslips break it down, person by person. Archon's Event Linker agent fuses all three into one accurate financial event. In the sample report, the bank moves about fourteen thousand euros, while the true employer cost is over eighteen thousand — a twenty-eight percent gap. But that twenty-eight percent is only the employer's side. Add what the employee pays, and the real gap is far larger.

Here is where that money hides. Start from the bank transfer — the number most owners book as payroll. Add back the income tax and social security withheld from employees, and you reach gross pay. Add the employer's own contributions on top, and you reach the true cost of employment. Across the corpus, the employer side alone adds about thirty-five percent over the bank figure; the employee withholdings add another thirty-six. Together they understate payroll by seventy-one percent — one hundred and thirty-three thousand euros, invisible until the three documents are correlated.

Archon runs entirely on Nebius Serverless AI. Two on-demand CPU jobs do the heavy lifting — one extracts every document, one runs the financial analysis — and both shut themselves down the moment they finish. A CPU AI endpoint hosts the FastAPI backend that orchestrates them and submits the jobs through the Nebius Python SDK. The frontier models live in the Nebius Inference API: Qwen two-point-five Vision, with seventy-two billion parameters, reads scanned and Greek-language documents; and Llama three-point-three, seventy billion, writes the executive summary. Object storage and managed PostgreSQL hold the raw files and the structured results. There is no always-on GPU — the containers stay cheap CPU instances, and the heavy compute lives in the inference layer.

We don't just claim accuracy — we measure it. Archon ships an evaluation harness, and it scores the real pipeline agents — the same Classifier, Event Linker, Validator, and P&L agents the product deploys, imported directly, not re-implemented — against a labelled corpus of forty Greek payroll cases. Every extracted number, date, classification, and fused total is checked against ground truth. One extractor reads the fields perfectly, to set the ceiling; a second, deliberately degraded extractor adds noise, to prove the metrics actually move. And the figure truth is scored separately from the validation truth, so a validation bug can never hide behind a correct number.

The results are concrete. Under perfect extraction, classification, field, and fusion accuracy all reach one hundred percent — the employer cost is reported to the cent, every time, across forty diverse cases. Validation outcomes land at almost ninety-seven percent, and that gap is deliberate — it points at a real defect we will come to. Now degrade the extractor: field accuracy falls into the seventies, but fusion accuracy collapses much faster, into the fifties, because small per-field errors compound through the fusion sums. That is exactly the signal a real extractor should be optimised against — and it is why the classifier earns its place.

The harness also earns its place — it surfaced a real defect. Archon advertises four cross-document checks. Rules one and three are active: the bank net must match the sum of payslip nets within two percent, and the payment date must fall inside the pay period. But rules two and four never fire — not on a single case, even under perfect extraction — because the register fields they read are never extracted. Take a payroll where the register claims more people than there are payslips: rule one still catches it on the figures, but rule four, the headcount check, simply skips, and silently passes a broken close. That is a number, not a hunch.

And every one of these numbers is reproducible from the README alone. The harness runs completely offline — no API key, no cloud, only pydantic — and scores the full forty-case corpus in about six seconds on a laptop, for zero euros, because the extractors are deterministic. Seed seven makes it byte-for-byte repeatable. The whole project sits at eighty-nine point eight percent test coverage, gated in continuous integration at a hard eighty-five percent floor — the harness, and the agents it scores, are themselves under test.
