Every month, a small business closes its books — and the truth is scattered across dozens of documents that never quite agree. Invoices, receipts, bank statements, payroll. Archon pulls all of it into one consolidated financial picture — a period-over-period P&L, EBITDA, the real cost of the workforce, and cash — then cross-checks the whole thing to surface what is missing or does not reconcile.

Take one example — the workforce. A single payroll event leaves three documents, each telling only part of the story. The bank confirmation shows the net transferred; the payroll register holds the full employer cost; the payslips break it down person by person. Archon's Event Linker fuses all three into one accurate event. In the sample, the bank moves about ten thousand seven hundred euros, but the true employer cost is over eighteen thousand — a gap of about seventy-two percent over the transfer. Half of that is the employer's own social-security contribution — roughly thirty-five percent.

Here is where the money hides. Start from the bank transfer. Add back the tax and social security withheld from employees to reach gross pay; add the employer's own contributions to reach the true cost of employment. Across the corpus, the employer side adds about thirty-five percent, the employee withholdings another thirty-six — together understating workforce cost by about seventy-two percent: one hundred and thirty-three thousand euros, invisible until the three documents are correlated.

Archon runs entirely on Nebius Serverless AI. Two on-demand CPU jobs do the heavy lifting — one extracts every document, one runs the analysis — and both shut down the moment they finish. A CPU AI endpoint hosts the FastAPI backend that orchestrates them and submits the jobs through the Nebius Python SDK. The frontier models live in the Nebius Inference API: Qwen two-point-five Vision reads scanned and multilingual documents, and Llama three-point-three writes the executive summary. There is no always-on GPU — the containers stay cheap CPU instances, and the heavy compute lives in the inference layer.

We don't just claim accuracy — we measure it. Archon ships an evaluation harness that scores the real pipeline agents — the same agents the product deploys, imported directly, not re-implemented — against a labelled corpus of forty synthetic cases. Every extracted number, date, and fused total is checked against ground truth. One extractor reads the fields perfectly, to set the ceiling; a second, deliberately degraded, adds noise to prove the metrics move. And figure accuracy is scored separately from validation, so a validation bug can never hide behind a correct number.

The results are concrete. Under perfect extraction, classification, field, and fusion accuracy all reach one hundred percent — the employer cost reported to the cent, across forty cases. Validation outcomes reach one hundred percent too, but only after the harness caught a real defect. Now degrade the extractor: field accuracy falls into the seventies, but fusion accuracy collapses faster, into the fifties, because small per-field errors compound through the fusion sums.

This is the completeness engine. Archon runs cross-document checks that reconcile independent sources. Rule one reconciles the bank net against the sum of the payslip nets — the same class of check that, extended across the ledger, catches a payment with no matching invoice. Rule three confirms the payment date falls in the pay period. And here the harness earned its place: rules two and four used to never fire — zero out of thirty-seven — because the register fields they read were never extracted, so a headcount mismatch slipped through silently. We wired those fields in, and the same harness now measures thirty-seven out of thirty-seven — proven, before and after.

And every number is reproducible from the README alone. The harness runs completely offline — no API key, only pydantic — and scores the full forty-case corpus in about six seconds on a laptop, for zero euros, because the extractors are deterministic. Seed seven makes it byte-for-byte repeatable. The whole project sits above ninety percent test coverage, gated in continuous integration at a hard eighty-five percent floor.
