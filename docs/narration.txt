Every month, a business owner asks the same questions. Did we pay everyone and everything we owe — and pay it correctly? Do the books reconcile, or did something slip? The answers are scattered across invoices, receipts, bank statements and payroll that never quite agree. Archon consolidates all of it into one financial picture — a period-over-period P&L, EBITDA, the real cost of the workforce, and cash — then cross-checks every source to confirm the numbers reconcile and to flag anything that does not.

Start with payroll. As an employer you pay three things every month: net wages to your people, the payroll taxes withheld on their behalf, and your own social-security contributions. A single payroll run leaves three documents that each tell only part of the story — the bank confirmation of the net transfer, the payroll register with the full employer cost, and the payslips, person by person. Archon's Event Linker fuses all three and answers the real question: did every component the register says you owe actually get paid? In the sample, the bank moved about ten thousand seven hundred euros of net wages — but the register's true employer cost is over eighteen thousand, once withheld taxes and employer contributions are included. Archon reconciles each part back to a source document, so nothing is missed and nothing is assumed.

That reconciliation is the point. The bank transfer alone is only the net wages. Add the tax and social security withheld from employees, then the employer's own contributions, and you arrive at the true cost of employment on the register. Archon walks that chain for every period — confirming the withholdings were remitted, the contributions were paid, and the components sum to the register total. In the sample those parts come to about seventy-two percent on top of the bank figure — every euro accounted for and tied to a document, so a missed remittance or a headcount that does not match cannot slip through.

The same completeness logic runs on the payables side. Archon reconciles each supplier's statement of account against your own invoices and payments — and tells you what needs attention: a supplier that has not been paid in full, a duplicate payment made by mistake, a payment for the wrong amount, or one sent to the wrong vendor. The questions an owner actually asks, answered from the documents.

Archon runs entirely on Nebius Serverless AI. Two on-demand CPU jobs do the heavy lifting — one extracts every document, one runs the analysis — and both shut down the moment they finish. A CPU AI endpoint hosts the FastAPI backend that orchestrates them and submits the jobs through the Nebius Python SDK. The frontier models live in the Nebius Inference API: Qwen two-point-five Vision reads scanned and multilingual documents, and Llama three-point-three writes the executive summary. There is no always-on GPU — the containers stay cheap CPU instances, and the heavy compute lives in the inference layer.

We don't just claim accuracy — we measure it. Archon ships an evaluation harness that scores the real pipeline agents — the same agents the product deploys, imported directly, not re-implemented — against a labelled corpus of forty synthetic cases. Every extracted number, date, and fused total is checked against ground truth. One extractor reads the fields perfectly, to set the ceiling; a second, deliberately degraded, adds noise to prove the metrics move. And figure accuracy is scored separately from validation, so a validation bug can never hide behind a correct number.

The results are concrete. Under perfect extraction, classification, field, and fusion accuracy all reach one hundred percent — the employer cost reconciled to the cent, across forty cases. Validation outcomes reach one hundred percent too, but only after the harness caught a real defect. Now degrade the extractor: field accuracy falls into the seventies, but fusion accuracy collapses faster, into the fifties, because small per-field errors compound through the reconciliation sums.

This is the completeness engine. Archon runs cross-document checks that reconcile independent sources. Rule one reconciles the bank net against the sum of the payslip nets — the same class of check that, extended across the ledger, catches a supplier payment with no matching invoice. Rule three confirms the payment date falls in the pay period. And here the harness earned its place: rules two and four used to never fire — zero out of thirty-seven — because the register fields they read were never extracted, so a headcount mismatch slipped through silently. We wired those fields in, and the same harness now measures thirty-seven out of thirty-seven — proven, before and after.

And every number is reproducible from the README alone. The harness runs completely offline — no API key, only pydantic — and scores the full forty-case corpus in about six seconds on a laptop, for zero euros, because the extractors are deterministic. Seed seven makes it byte-for-byte repeatable. The whole project sits above ninety percent test coverage, gated in continuous integration at a hard eighty-five percent floor.
